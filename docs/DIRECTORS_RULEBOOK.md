# R4 — Director's Rulebook: Craft (v1, 13 Oct 2026)

Scope: AI-assisted animated/cinematic pieces, 15 s–12 min. f = frames at 24 fps (≈42 ms); FF = full-frame-equivalent mm; ASL = average shot length; **(house)** = studio default, not research. §9 = blockers; others are majors, waivable with a written reason.

## 1. STORY

R-1-01 | Hook ≤3 s: the first shot poses a question, anomaly or threat; no logo or title first. | WHY: 47% of a mobile video's value lands in 3 s, 74% in 10 s [Meta]. | CHECK: Freeze at 72 f: open question statable; no logo/title yet.

R-1-02 | Pay the promise (genre + reward for staying) by 10% of runtime, matching title and thumbnail. | WHY: YouTube's Intro metric (% watching at 30 s) is high when openings match title/thumbnail [YT]. | CHECK: Promise paid ≤10% in; thumbnail claim shown within 30 s.

R-1-03 | Beat map. 15 s: hook, twist, button. 60 s: hook 0–3 s, setup to 15 s, escalation to 45 s, turn, button in last 3–5 s. 3–5 min: compressed Save the Cat (catalyst ≈11%, break into two ≈23%, midpoint 50%, all-is-lost ≈68%, break into three ≈77%). 8–12 min: Harmon's eight steps (you, need, go, search, find, take, return, change). | WHY: Snyder: pp. 12/25/55/75/85 of 110 [Snyder]; Harmon built the circle for TV [Harmon]. | CHECK: Beat markers within ±5% of runtime of target.

R-1-04 | Beats connect by "but" or "therefore", never "and then". | WHY: Parker & Stone's causality test [P&S]. | CHECK: Insert connectors between beat sentences; any "and then" = rewrite.

R-1-05 | Protagonist shows a concrete want within 20%, holds opinions, acts; stakes visible. | WHY: Pixar #1, #13, #16: trying, opinions, stakes [Coats]. | CHECK: Want is an on-screen goal; own actions ≥ actions suffered.

R-1-06 | Before boards: logline ≤25 words, ending and final image written; final image rhymes with or inverts the opening. | WHY: Pixar #7, #22; Snyder's image pair proves change. | CHECK: First/last frames side by side; change nameable in ≤10 words.

R-1-07 | Every payoff has an on-screen setup ≥15% of runtime earlier; coincidence may cause trouble, never solve it; unpaid setups become logged series threads. | WHY: Pixar #19; orphans read as errors (gap: house). | CHECK: Setup/payoff table with shot IDs.

R-1-08 | Keep ≥1 question open until the last 10%; open the next loop before closing the current; end episodes on an open question. | WHY: Curiosity is a felt knowledge gap [Loewenstein]; the Zeigarnik memory effect failed meta-analysis [Ghibellini]. | CHECK: Question ledger; any zero-open interval = fail.

R-1-09 | Micro-turn (reveal, reversal, escalation) every ≤15–20 s in short form, ≤45–60 s at 8–12 min. | WHY: Retention dips mark where viewers leave [YT]; cadence (house). | CHECK: Max gap between marked turns.

R-1-10 | Series: recurring characters have a black-fill-readable silhouette, signature want, tic and never-healed flaw; episodes close their A-story and move one arc thread; cold open ≤10%. | WHY: Recognition brings viewers back (house). | CHECK: Silhouette test; arc beat in series bible.

## 2. SHOT GRAMMAR

R-2-01 | Size = information and intimacy: EWS/WS place, scale, isolation; FS/MS action; MCU/CU thought, emotion; ECU the decisive detail; tighten toward the emotional peak. | WHY: Closer framing reads more subjective [Mascelli; Katz]. | CHECK: Peak uses the scene's tightest size or a declared isolating wide.

R-2-02 | Lens = emotion (FF mm): 14–24 space, unease up close; 35–50 neutral; 85–135 intimacy, compression; ≥200 surveillance. | WHY: Focal length dictates camera distance, hence perspective [Brown]. | CHECK: Lens declared per shot; no unintended face distortion in CUs.

R-2-03 | Every move is motivated: following motion, revealing information, or externalizing an inner change. | WHY: Unmotivated moves are decoration [Brown; Katz]. | CHECK: Each moving shot names its motivation.

R-2-04 | Move meanings: push-in realization, tension; pull-out isolation, ending; zoom noticing, stylized; handheld anxiety; crane up scale, reveal; static authority, dread, deadpan; dolly-zoom unreality; whip pan energy. | WHY: Learned conventions [Brown; Katz]. | CHECK: Move matches the beat's emotion tag; push vs zoom verified by parallax.

R-2-05 | Hold the 180° line and screen direction (default L→R progress, R→L return); cross only on camera, via a neutral head-on shot or a re-establishing wide. Eyelines converge across shot/reverse; show the looker before a POV. | WHY: Positions, direction and gaze carry geography [Mascelli; Smith]. | CHECK: Overhead diagram, direction arrows, paired gaze vectors; unexplained flip = fail.

R-2-06 | Same-subject cuts need ≥30° angle change and/or ≥1 size step; otherwise it's a jump cut (declared style only). | WHY: Near-identical framings read as a glitch or regeneration. | CHECK: Angle/size compared at every cut.

R-2-07 | Compose in depth: a foreground element in ≥30% of narrative shots; eyes on the upper third; in CU crop the crown, not the chin; lead room toward gaze/motion, removed only to trap a character. | WHY: Depth staging layers information [Katz; Bordwell]; classical framing [Mascelli; Brown]. | CHECK: Count ≥3-plane shots (house); thirds overlay, eyes within ±5% of the line.

R-2-08 | Aspect: 2.39:1 scope/trailer, 16:9 YouTube, 9:16 recomposed (never letterboxed): MS or tighter ≥60%, vertical stacking, tilts over pans; faces/text out of top 15%, bottom 35% (45% under long TikTok captions), left 11%, right 22%. | WHY: Platform UI covers these zones (published templates; verify) [Safe]. | CHECK: Mask overlay on every 9:16 frame.

## 3. EDITING RHYTHM

R-3-01 | Cut by emotion, story, rhythm, eye-trace, 2D plane, 3D space, in that order; sacrifice from the bottom. | WHY: Murch weights them 51/23/10/7/5/4% [Murch]. | CHECK: Flagged cuts name the criterion served.

R-3-02 | Cut on action mid-gesture, or at a breakpoint (action complete, look landed, exit). | WHY: Motion hides cuts [Smith]; viewers' blinks synchronize at breakpoints [Nakano]. | CHECK: No skipped/doubled action phase >4 f; ≥80% of drama cuts qualify (house).

R-3-03 | Eye-trace: incoming focus within ~1/3 frame width of outgoing focus unless jolting on purpose. | WHY: Murch; trailer editors hold focus position across cuts [Lieu]. | CHECK: Mark and measure focus on last/first frames.

R-3-04 | ASL by energy: dread 6–12 s; dialogue 4–6 s; adventure/animation 3–5 s; action 2–4 s; trailer escalation 0.5–2 s. | WHY: Films average ≈4 s, varying in 1/f patterns [Cutting]; Bourne Ultimatum ≈2 s [Bordwell]; action 4.0 s [Follows]. | CHECK: ASL per sequence from EDL; shot-length CV ≥0.4 (house).

R-3-05 | Pacing curve: slow setup, accelerating escalation, a long hold just before the climax, release; never plateau at peak. | WHY: "Lows don't look low unless there's a high" [Lieu]. | CHECK: Shot-length plot accelerates, then decelerates pre-climax.

R-3-06 | Silence before impact: 0.5–2 s of room tone before the biggest hit, reveal or title. | WHY: Pauses drop arousal below baseline [Bernardi]; the hit lands harder. | CHECK: ≥12 f at ≥20 dB under the next hit (house).

R-3-07 | Split edits (J/L, 6–24 f) on ≥50% of dialogue cuts and scene transitions. | WHY: Sound motivates and smooths the cut (house). | CHECK: EDL audio offsets; no clicks.

R-3-08 | Hold faces when emotion lands: reactions ≥24 f, turning points 48–96 f. | WHY: Facial mimicry differentiates emotions only after ~300–500 ms [Dimberg]. | CHECK: Reaction durations from EDL.

R-3-09 | Cut by default; match cuts for meaning; dissolves only for time or memory; no preset spins, zoom-blurs, light leaks or glitches unless bible-listed. | WHY: Templates read as stock (house). | CHECK: Each non-cut transition has a bible entry.

R-3-10 | Montage: each shot a distinct step of change; consistent on/off-beat cutting; landing shot ≥1.5× montage ASL. | WHY: Montage compresses time by progression, not repetition (house). | CHECK: No repeated state; landing length.

## 4. LIGHTING & COLOR

R-4-01 | Every light has a named in-world source; night and interior shots show ≥1 practical. | WHY: Motivated light keeps the world credible [Brown]. | CHECK: Key source in shot list; shadows fall away from it; practical present.

R-4-02 | Separate subject from background by rim/back light or ≥1-stop edge difference. | WHY: Readability at phone size (house). | CHECK: Passes in ≥90% of shots.

R-4-03 | Contrast (key+fill):fill: 2:1 high key, comedy, kids; 4:1 low-key drama; 8:1+ chiaroscuro, noir, horror. | WHY: The ratio defines the key [UT; Brown]. | CHECK: Face key/fill luminance in the bible band.

R-4-04 | Silhouettes: background ≥3 stops over subject, rim on the readable side, limbs clear of the body. | WHY: Poses must read in silhouette [T&J]. | CHECK: 1-bit threshold still reads.

R-4-05 | Color script first: one thumbnail per beat mapping hue, value, saturation to the arc; palettes shift at turns. | WHY: Pixar color-scripts every film; Toy Story's pastels go muddy when things turn sinister [Pixar]. | CHECK: Cut matches the script beat by beat.

R-4-06 | One dominant palette per scene plus one accent reserved for meaning; defaults below. | WHY: Color–emotion links are largely shared across 30 nations (red = love and anger) [Jonauskaite]. | CHECK: Accent only on bible-assigned elements.

R-4-07 | One grade philosophy per project: teal–orange (subtle; overused), bleach bypass (desaturated, hard), noir (crushed blacks, mono or one accent), cel (flat shadow, one highlight, hue-shifted shadows), painterly (brush texture, painted light). | WHY: Mixed logics read as stock; teal–orange became a DI cliché [Miro]. | CHECK: Project LUT on every shot; histograms in band.

R-4-08 | Film cues are seasoning: identical grain on every shot, halation only on highlights, vignette ≤0.5 stop, aberration only at edges, flares only with a visible source. | WHY: Heavy presets read as cliché and expose composites (house). | CHECK: Grain everywhere; corner falloff ≤0.5 stop.

Palette defaults: joy = warm yellow/gold, high value; love = red, rose, soft key; anger/danger = red-orange on black, hard light; sadness = desaturated blue-grey, flat; fear = cyan-green, sick yellow, black, 8:1+; awe = deep blue-violet, one warm point; corruption = olive, bile green, murky; memory = amber, sepia, lifted blacks.

## 5. ANIMATION

12 principles [T&J] (2.5D; 3D; AI): squash/stretch = mesh-warp; volume-keeping deformers; stylized only. Anticipation = counter-rotation; wind-up key; wind-up in start frame. Staging = clear silhouette; blocking; composed reference frame. Pose-to-pose = key poses; blocking→splines; start/end-frame conditioning. Overlap = layers offset 2–4 f; offset hair/cloth; check settles. Slow in/out, arcs = eases, pivots; tangents; reject glide. Secondary action = blink, breath, fidget (AI: prompt it). Timing = 2s/3s holds; 1s; retime AI in edit. Exaggeration = push poses (AI: in references, never morphs). Solid drawing = turnarounds; rig integrity; identity lock. Appeal = clear asymmetric shapes; reject uncanny symmetry.

R-5-01 | Every deliberate action: anticipation (counter-move 4–12 f), action, follow-through/settle; organic motion eases and arcs; key poses read in silhouette. | WHY: [T&J]; AI starts actions cold and glides (range: house). | CHECK: Frame-step for counter-moves; tracked speed curves ease; key poses as black fills.

R-5-02 | Frame rate is language: stylized characters on 2s, 1s for fast action and camera moves; realistic 3D/AI on 1s; rate can track growth. | WHY: Spider-Verse used twos for crispness [Beveridge], moving Miles to 1s as he gained confidence [Substack]; Arcane's hand-drawn FX run at 12 fps [RedShark]. | CHECK: Unique frames/s match the declared rate.

R-5-03 | Moves crossing frame in ≤4 f get 1–2 f smears/multiples (stylized) or 180°-shutter blur (realistic). | WHY: Smears sell speed (Arcane's Jinx) [RedShark]; strobing reads as error. | CHECK: Every fast move has smear or blur.

R-5-04 | Secondary motion lags 2–4 f and settles over 6–12 f; nothing stops dead. | WHY: Overlapping action [T&J]; ranges (house). | CHECK: Frame-step 12 f after each stop.

R-5-05 | Blinks: regular 2/1/3 f (close/closed/open), fast 2/0/3 (anxiety), long 3/2/4 (thought change); on head turns and thought shifts; 8–25/min in calm dialogue. Thinking eyes dart (3–4 f) every 0.5–2 s; catchlights on the key side. | WHY: Animation practice [Bloop]; real blinks last 100–400 ms at ~15–20/min [Blink]. | CHECK: Blinks/min per face; blink on turns >45°; dart or blink every 2 s in CUs.

R-5-06 | No dead holds: breathing cycle 3–5 s, weight shifts every 4–8 s. | WHY: Adults breathe 12–20 times/min; frozen figures read as paused video. | CHECK: Holds >2 s show torso motion.

R-5-07 | Weight: planted feet slide ≤1 px at 1080p (≤3 stylized); contact shadows on every contact; heavy masses start slow, overshoot, settle. | WHY: Weight reads through contact; foot-skate is a top CG/AI tell (house). | CHECK: Track planted-foot pixels.

R-5-08 | Fix AI failures (floating, foot-skate, morphing, cold starts, glide, frozen listeners): generate ≤5 s, cut on action; condition on start/end frames and pose reference; lock identity with references; regenerate or inpaint; cut before the failure frame; never interpolate to smooth. | WHY: Video models' physics is weak and unrelated to realism [Physics-IQ]. | CHECK: Shot log (model, seed, references); passes §9.

## 6. GAME CINEMATIC TRAILER GRAMMAR

Phase map (% of runtime; at 120 s): cold open 0–5% (0–6 s), most distinctive image or line; quiet build 5–35% (6–42 s), world, face, want, sparse score; turn 35–45% (42–54 s), a reframing reveal, hit; escalation 45–80% (54–96 s), shorter shots on beats, risers; crescendo 80–90% (96–108 s), hero moment; silence ≈90%, 0.5–2 s; title stinger 90–95%, logo, hit, sub, tail; tag/button 95–100%, line or reveal, date/CTA.

Anchors (beat order verified; timecodes unverified, YouTube unreachable):
- Cyberpunk 2077 (E3 2019): V delivers a chip → turn: flashback to Jackie's death → ambush, shootout → blackout → button: Johnny Silverhand, "Wake the f— up, Samurai. We have a city to burn." [PCGames]
- Overwatch Cinematic (Blizzard 2014, 5:48): slow museum-tour build → Widowmaker and Reaper break in → Tracer and Winston fight. [SIGGRAPH; Wiki-OW]
- Diablo IV "By Three They Come" (2019): hunters find a carved "three" → turn: they are the offering → their blood births Lilith; "By three they come… Blood is the key." [PCGamer-D4]
- Witcher 3 "Killing Monsters" (Platige 2013): grey winter countryside; moral turn, "who the real monster is." [CGChannel; Siliconera]

R-6-01 | Build on the phase map; peak, then stop: title/date right after the crescendo, then the button. | WHY: Lieu's cold open, intro, escalation, climax, button [Lieu]. | CHECK: Phases within ±5%; title never before 85%.

R-6-02 | Cold open = the most distinctive image or line; logos wait unless famous. | WHY: Start strong, no exposition [Lieu]. | CHECK: Hero element in first 72 f.

R-6-03 | Quiet build: sparse or diegetic sound, ASL ≥3 s, ≥1 CU of want or vulnerability. | WHY: Spectacle needs a face first. | CHECK: Build ASL; music ≥6 dB under crescendo (house).

R-6-04 | The turn reframes what we were watching. | WHY: Recontextualization drives every anchor. | CHECK: Reviewer completes "I thought X; now Y."

R-6-05 | Escalation: each shot outdoes the last and adds something new; lengths trend to 0.5–1.5 s; ≥80% of cuts on beats ±2 f. | WHY: Shorter cuts plus variety [Lieu]. | CHECK: Negative length slope; beat alignment.

R-6-06 | Hero moment: camera below eye line, rim light, silhouette over the brightest area, slow motion or hold ≥36 f. | WHY: Low angle + silhouette + time = power (house). | CHECK: Camera height; silhouette; duration.

R-6-07 | Sound: risers end on hits; hits with sub-drops on cuts ±1 f; whooshes on fast moves; tails bridge cuts; ticking or Shepard-tone drones build tension; ≤2 braams; 0.5–2 s silence before the title. | WHY: Effects land on the music [Jerden]; braams are a parodied Inception-era cliché [BRAAAM]; Dunkirk's tension rides ticking and Shepard tones [Dunkirk]. | CHECK: SFX cue sheet vs EDL; braam count; pre-title drop ≥12 f.

R-6-08 | The logo forms from one world element (blood, fire, light), holds ≥48 f with hit, sub and tail; the tag reopens the story; one CTA. | WHY: Cyberpunk's Silverhand button; one CTA avoids decision paralysis [Lieu]. | CHECK: Legible at 360p; hold; single CTA.

## 7. STORYBOARD & ANIMATIC

R-7-01 | Beat board first: 6–12 frames that tell the story silently. | WHY: Visualize before committing [Katz]. | CHECK: Reviewer retells the logline from the board alone.

R-7-02 | Shot list, every field mandatory: ID (EP-SC-SH), beat, duration (s+f), size, angle/height, lens, move/motivation, start/end pose, dialogue/VO, SFX/music, axis side/direction, light source, grade ID, continuity anchors, transitions, AI fields (model, prompt ID, seed, references, start/end frames), status, QC result. | WHY: QC tests only what is declared. | CHECK: No blanks; durations sum to target ±2%.

R-7-03 | Lock VO (or timed scratch) before animating; time everything to it. | WHY: Lip-sync and holds depend on it. | CHECK: Line spans = VO ±2 f.

R-7-04 | Panels hold true screen time with temp music, key SFX and drawn moves; one idea per panel. | WHY: Pacing only shows in real time. | CHECK: Runtime = target ±5%.

R-7-05 | Review: one non-stop pass logging drift points, then in order: reads on mute? on audio only? emotion lands? pacing curve? geography? eye-trace? every shot survives the delete test? hook and promise on time? | WHY: Stopping hides dips (house); emotion and story come first [Murch]. | CHECK: Log shows the no-stop pass and each item.

R-7-06 | Lock at runtime ±5%, review passed, VO locked, no TBD shots; later changes need a written request; generate exact frame counts plus 6–12 f handles. | WHY: Pre-lock generation wastes compute and breaks continuity. | CHECK: Lock record; handles ≥6 f.

R-7-07 | Continuity bible: turnarounds, costume per scene, props, set plans with camera positions, light map. | WHY: Generations start from zero; references are their memory. | CHECK: Every visible character/prop cites a bible ID.

## 8. EMOTION & PSYCHOLOGY

R-8-01 | Tag each beat with a Plutchik emotion (joy, trust, fear, surprise, sadness, disgust, anger, anticipation) and intensity (apprehension→fear→terror); alternate contrasts. | WHY: Shared, graded vocabulary [Plutchik]; emotion is 51% of a cut [Murch]. | CHECK: Tag per beat; no flat stretch >30 s (house).

R-8-02 | Readable eyes at every emotional peak (CU/MCU, both eyes, ≥24 f). | WHY: Gaze converges on faces [Coutrot]; facial mimicry within ~300–500 ms, not contested "mirror neurons," is the reliable mechanism [Dimberg]. | CHECK: Qualifying shot per peak.

R-8-03 | Macro-expressions hold 0.5–4 s; micro-expressions (1/25–1/5 s) only as a deliberate leak. | WHY: Ekman's range; upper limit ≈0.5 s [MicroSurvey]. | CHECK: Unplanned expression flicker <12 f (AI artifact) = fail.

R-8-04 | Curiosity and reward: make the gap noticed (withheld object, off-screen sound, partial reveal), stretch the wait, then pay bigger or differently than predicted. | WHY: Curiosity needs a perceived gap [Loewenstein]; dopamine tracks reward prediction error [Schultz]. | CHECK: Gap shot ≥3 s before each reveal; payoff beats expectation.

R-8-05 | Tempo tracks arousal; a true pause precedes the climax. | WHY: Tempo drove breathing and cardiovascular arousal; pauses dropped it below baseline [Bernardi]; literal heartbeat entrainment is weaker evidence. | CHECK: Cue-sheet tempo curve parallels the emotion curve.

R-8-06 | Awe: small figure against vast scale (≥1:20), reveal ≥4 s, near-silence, then a reacting face. | WHY: Vastness plus need for accommodation [Keltner & Haidt]. | CHECK: Scale reference, reveal length, reaction shot.

R-8-07 | Horror: dread dominates; ≤1 jump scare per ~5 min, each after ≥10 s of build (silence, negative space, off-screen sound); ≤1 false scare per real one; sound and image on one frame. | WHY: Features level off near 10 jump scares [WTJ]; scares spike heart rate (Sinister: 86 bpm average, 131 peak) [Scare]. | CHECK: Scare count, build length; ≥40% negative space in dread shots (house).

R-8-08 | Comedy: rule of three; a beat before the punchline (12–36 f in animation); deadpan = static camera, no sting; reaction = hold 6–12 f, cut to the face. | WHY: Pre-punchline pauses stay consistent across performances [Pope]; frames (house). | CHECK: Pause measured.

R-8-09 | Pathos: the climax is an on-screen choice that sacrifices the want for the need. | WHY: Harmon's "take" step; Pixar #1. | CHECK: Want, need, cost named; choice shown, not narrated.

## 9. ANTI-SLOP CHECKLIST (blockers)

R-9-01 | No flicker, texture swim, boiling or melting. | WHY: Consistency and flicker are core VBench dimensions [VBench]. | CHECK: 25%-speed scrub; frame-to-frame DINO/CLIP similarity ≥ threshold.

R-9-02 | Hands and faces hold: five fingers, stable joints, real grip contact; same face, eyes, scars, teeth across shots. | WHY: Anatomical errors lead AI tells [Kamali]. | CHECK: Freeze every 6 f; face-crop grid and embedding similarity per character.

R-9-03 | Physics: gravity accelerates, liquids fall, wind is consistent, shadows and reflections match sources, nothing interpenetrates. | WHY: Realism ≠ physics [Physics-IQ]; wrong shadows and empty reflections are tells [TechRadar]. | CHECK: Per-shot physics pass.

R-9-04 | Continuity: props, costume, injuries, light, time of day and screen direction persist across cuts. | WHY: AI inherits no state between shots. | CHECK: Continuity sheet vs every shot thumbnail.

R-9-05 | Motion has life: eases, impacts, micro-variation, camera inertia; ≥1 deliberate hold ≥24 f per minute; major actions anticipated. | WHY: Floaty, linear motion is a tell [Opus]; AI keeps everything mid-motion. | CHECK: Tracked speed curves accelerate; no interpolation fixes; hold count; R-5-01.

R-9-06 | No default AI frame (centered eye-level MS, bokeh, golden backlight): no size/angle/position combination >20% of shots. | WHY: Sameness and flat light are tells [Opus]. | CHECK: Shot classification histogram.

R-9-07 | No generated text: signs and screens blank, defocused, or real type comped in. | WHY: Garbled text is a top giveaway [PopSci]. | CHECK: OCR; non-words or mutating glyphs = fail.

R-9-08 | Scale holds: heights vs doors, props, each other. | WHY: Impossible proportions are a tell [TechRadar]. | CHECK: Height vs fixed reference, variance ≤5% (house).

R-9-09 | Eyes alive: catchlights, natural blinks, darts, convergence on target. | WHY: Missing micro-motion is uncanny [Mori]. | CHECK: ≥1 blink per 10 s of face time; catchlights present.

R-9-10 | Slow push-ins ≤25% of shots; locked-off ≥30%; every move motivated. | WHY: Default push-ins become wallpaper (house). | CHECK: Move histogram.

R-9-11 | Sound is designed: score changes at turns, hits land on picture, no loop repeats >2×, all licensed; ≥1 deliberate silence (≥12 f, ≥20 dB) per minute; room tone under dialogue. | WHY: Music ignoring cuts sounds stock [Jerden]; no contrast, no impact [Bernardi]. | CHECK: Cue sheet, loop scan, license file, waveform scan.

R-9-12 | Edit has rhythm and causality: never ≥4 consecutive equal-length shots (±2 f) unless musical (CV ≥0.4); no "vibe montage" of unconnected pretty shots. | WHY: Fixed generation lengths make metronomic cuts; causality separates film from wallpaper. | CHECK: EDL analysis; but/therefore test (R-1-04).

R-9-13 | Voice prosody: pitch range, stressed key words, breaths, pace following emotion; consecutive lines never share one contour. | WHY: Missing breaths and flat "TTS plateau" delivery are tells [Opus]. | CHECK: Listening pass plus F0 contours.

R-9-14 | Lip-sync within ATSC IS-191 (lead ≤15 ms, lag ≤45 ms): sound never a frame early, ≤1 f late; p/b/m close the lips. | WHY: Offsets become detectable from ≈45 ms lead/125 ms lag [AV]. | CHECK: Frame-step plosives; sync tool.

R-9-15 | Rights and platform (grey area, flag to owner): disclose realistic synthetic content where required; no near-identical cross-episode templates (YouTube demonetizes mass-produced, repetitive content); no living artists' names in prompts; license all music, logos, likenesses. | WHY: Can demonetize or remove work; verify current policy each release. | CHECK: Signed upload checklist.

## 10. STYLE SHEET TEMPLATE

R-10-01 | Palette: 5–8 swatches with hex, role, max frame share; accent bound to story elements; market exceptions listed (white = mourning in China). | WHY: Locks the look; color meaning varies by culture [Jonauskaite]. | CHECK: Sampled frames within ΔE ≤10 of a swatch; accent under cap.

R-10-02 | Line & material: line weight/ink, texture, finish, edges. | WHY: Consistent materials make generations one film. | CHECK: Prompts cite the material block.

R-10-03 | Lens set & format: focal lengths with uses, DOF, aspect per deliverable. | WHY: A constrained lens set is a voice. | CHECK: Shot lenses ⊂ set.

R-10-04 | Lighting key: contrast band, key direction, practicals, color temperatures. | WHY: Light carries mood fastest. | CHECK: Ratios in band.

R-10-05 | Motion: frame/hold rates, allowed moves with max share, handheld amplitude, transitions. | WHY: AI defaults erase motion style first. | CHECK: Move histogram vs rules.

R-10-06 | Typography: licensed faces, sizes for 16:9 and 9:16, grid, safe zones. | WHY: Legibility and brand. | CHECK: Legible at 360p and on a phone.

R-10-07 | Sound: instruments, tempo band, motif, ambiences, SFX families, VO spec (timbre, wpm, breaths), loudness ≈ −14 LUFS / ≤ −1 dBTP (verify per platform). | WHY: Sound defines the world. | CHECK: Loudness meter; cue sheet vs palette.

R-10-08 | Forbidden list: banned looks, moves, sounds, references, legal risks. | WHY: Saying no keeps a style. | CHECK: QC scans every shot.

**Filled example: "Ink & Ember", motion-comic noir for dark historical storytelling**
- Palette: Ink `#0B0B0D` base 45–60%; Night Navy `#141A26` shadows; Slate `#3B4654` mids; Bone `#D9CFBC` highlights ≤15%; Sea Teal `#1F4E5A` moon/water ≤15%; Sodium Amber `#C98A2E` practicals ≤10%; Ember Red `#9E1B1B` blood/fire/danger only, ≤3%.
- Material: 2.5D cut-out; brush-ink outlines 3–6 px at 1080p; paper grain; halftone shadows ≤20%; matte except wet eyes and blood.
- Lenses: 24 mm unease, 40 mm neutral, 85 mm faces, 135 mm surveillance; deep focus; 3–5 parallax layers.
- Light: 8:1–16:1; one hard motivated key (lantern, moon, fire, window); opposite rim; no fill on antagonists; slatted-shadow motif.
- Motion: characters on 2s, 1s for fast action and camera moves; locked-off ≥60%; pushes ≤20%; rare snap-zooms; handheld only in panic; ink-wipe transitions ≤1/min.
- Type: IM Fell English captions, Oswald titles (SIL OFL).
- Sound: cello/bass drones, sparse piano or solo voice, water/wind/timber beds, a ticking motif; silence before reveals; close-mic VO, 130–150 wpm, breaths kept.
- Forbidden: teal–orange, flares, braams, glossy skin, generated text, morph transitions. Real atrocity: imply, don't display; no gore close-ups or glamorized violence; victims are named people, not props; no sensational thumbnails of real suffering.
