## How an ad is made (the business pipeline)

1. **Brief.** Business, audience, the one idea, the end-card line, the policy (`unmissed`), the target length (15 to 30 s).
2. **Style board** (`board.yml`, about $0.10 per still). Two or three stills per look, same business, shown to Sam on one page.
   Sam picks. No video is paid for before this.
3. **Short test** of the chosen look (a 6 s clip at about $0.60, or a code render at $0).
4. **Production.**
   - Code-built ad: `codead.yml` (scene in `ads/<id>/`). Picture free; voice about $0.01; music about $0.05 to $0.08.
   - AI-footage ad: `piece.yml` (shot factory). About $3 to $10 at standard quality; cap US$10 per client ad.
   - Hybrid: AI stills or clips (from `board.yml`) placed inside a code-built scene (photos in letters, cut-out products).
5. **Check every frame** before Sam sees it: contact sheet plus a look at the moving clip. Reject warping people, hands,
   melting shapes, unreadable text, anything that reads as alcohol or pork for halal-friendly clients.
6. **Deliver** to Sam (release link, files in chat, the board page). He approves. Nothing is published or sent without him.

- **Text, logos and numbers are set in code**, never generated by an image or video model.

- **Public repo.** Invented businesses, Sam's own brands (Smart Mount is cleared) and studio code only. Client material
  will go to a private repo once there are paying clients (parked by Sam until then).

## Lessons learned (do not repeat)

- One look file used for every piece made everything look the same (cartoon 3D). Show a style board first, always.
- Automatic "score and redo" of video clips doubled the cost when the look itself was the problem. Fix the look, not the clip.
- A script crash lost paid stills: save every paid output to disk and upload it the moment it exists.
- The OpenRouter key's `usage` lags by hours. Per-call costs from the API (`usage.cost`) are the reliable figure.
- Lyria returns audio only when streamed (`stream: true`); Seedream needs `modalities: ["image"]`.
- Video models warp crowds of small particles and people's hands. They hold one solid object well.
- ffmpeg: `xfade` shorter than about 0.22 s drops segments; trim on the input (`-ss`/`-t` before `-i`); always `-nostdin`.
- Square stills fed to a 16:9 video model get pillarboxed: crop to the target aspect first.
- Masked word reveals need at least 135% travel or descenders peek out of the mask.
