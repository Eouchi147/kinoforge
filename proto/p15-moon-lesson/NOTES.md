# P15 — WHY THE MOON CHANGES SHAPE (62.5 s, 16:9, Blender Cycles CPU, animated ON 2s)

A clay classroom demonstration: a desk lamp is the Sun, a clay Earth on a turntable, a clay Moon on a bent wire arm, and a
thumb-sized clay kid standing on the Earth. Real light does the physics — the phases in the over-the-shoulder shots are what
the lamp actually lights, seen from the kid's spot. The orbit sits at the kid's eye line and the lamp 18 degrees above it,
so the full moon clears the Earth's shadow (the real Moon misses it thanks to its ~5 degree orbital tilt); the myth beat
lowers the Moon into the shadow on purpose to show that an eclipse is a different, rarer thing.

Exercises: beat timing from a narration timing file (timing.json), a camera that circles a subject to prove a point
(the hook), a crane-back reveal, a POV that follows a moving subject, a practical light switched off as a story beat,
light-linking discipline (the lit desk is hidden from diffuse rays so the Moon's night side stays dark), stop-motion
grammar shared with P10 (poses on 2s, per-pose jitter, pose-seeded grain, pair reuse).

Narration, score and sound are not part of the example (the voice is a separate recording).
