# Why Venice Sinks (Under the Surface, film 1): script

Tone: curious, upbeat, kid-clear, a glint of humour; one picture idea per line. About 330 words, 17 lines.
Facts checked on 22 Oct 2026 (sources at the end). Line `l01` is the title line.

| Line | Narration | Picture (one idea) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| l00 | A city of palaces, churches and bell towers, standing in the middle of the sea. Here is the strange part. Underneath it, there is almost no solid ground. | wide: a block of Venice floating in mist; push toward the cut face: water, then soft mud |
| l01 | Why Venice Sinks. | title over the block |
| l02 | About fifteen hundred years ago, people running from war hid on small muddy islands in a lagoon: a shallow bay, protected from the sea by a long strip of sand. | an empty lagoon block: reeds, mud flats, a sand bar; a few boats arrive |
| l03 | Mud is a terrible place to build. Put a stone house on it, and the house slowly sinks, like a spoon in pudding. | cutaway: a little stone house sinks into the mud (slow, a bit comic) |
| l04 | So the Venetians did something clever. They hammered wooden poles straight down into the mud. Millions of them. | poles drive down one by one, then many, seen through the cut face |
| l05 | Each pole was a tree trunk, sharpened at the end, of oak, alder or pine. They packed them tight, side by side, all the way down to a harder layer of clay. | close on the cut: a forest of poles, tips reaching a firmer pale clay band |
| l06 | On top of the poles they laid thick wooden boards. On the boards, waterproof white stone. And only then, the walls. | layers build up in order: boards, white stone, brick walls rising |
| l07 | One church alone, Santa Maria della Salute, stands on more than a million of these poles. | the dome on its block, the cut face packed with poles; a counter |
| l08 | But wait. Wood in water rots, right? Not down there. Deep in the mud there is almost no air, and the tiny creatures that eat wood need air to live. | close: a pole in dark mud, a few tiny bubbles stop; a drawn label "no air" |
| l09 | So for hundreds of years the poles have stayed strong, and minerals in the water have soaked into them and made them even harder. | the pole slowly turns stony grey |
| l10 | So why is Venice sinking? Two reasons. First, the mud under the city is still being squeezed, very slowly, by everything standing on top of it. | the mud layers compress a little; the whole block lowers a hair |
| l11 | Second, the sea is rising. A little more water every year, a slightly lower city. | water line creeps up the cut face against a scale bar |
| l12 | In the last century, factories nearby made it worse. They pumped water up from under the ground, the mud squeezed down, and the city dropped faster. Then the pumping was stopped. | pipes pull water from a deep layer; layers squeeze; the pipe shuts off |
| l13 | Today, on the worst days, the sea pours into the squares, and people walk to work on raised wooden walkways. | the square floods; small figures cross a walkway |
| l14 | So Venice built a wall that hides under the sea. Seventy-eight giant gates lie on the seabed at the three openings of the lagoon. When a big tide comes, air is pumped in, and they float up to hold the sea back. | a block of the lagoon mouth: gates on the seabed fill with air and swing up |
| l15 | They rose for real for the first time in October 2020. The squares stayed dry. | the square stays dry while the sea outside is high |
| l16 | A city standing on an upside down forest, in the middle of the sea, for fifteen hundred years. Just keep your feet dry. | final wide: the block at dusk, lights on, slow pull back |

## Facts and sources
- Poles of alder, oak and pine, 2 to 8 m long, 5 to 10 per square metre; oxygen-poor mud stops decay; minerals harden
  the wood over time; 20th-century groundwater pumping for industry sped up subsidence and was halted:
  pilebuck.com, "Building Venice: timber piles" (read 22 Oct 2026).
- The harder clay layer under the lagoon mud is the caranto (widely documented; not named on screen).
- Santa Maria della Salute on more than a million piles (the usual figure is 1,106,657): kept as "more than a million".
- MOSE: 78 gates, three inlets, rest on the lagoon floor and rise when air is pumped in; first raised against a high
  tide on 3 Oct 2020: Smithsonian Magazine (Oct 2020) and CNN.
- Founding about 1,500 years ago: refugees from the invasions of the 5th and 6th centuries (traditional date 421).
- Raised walkways at high water (acqua alta): long-standing practice.
